What should I do immediately if I suspect I am being followed on the street in India? +

If someone is following or stalking you on foot or on a two-wheeler, take these immediate actions:

  1. Do not proceed to your home or hostel: Never lead a stalker to your personal residence or deserted alleyway.
  2. Cross the street or turn into populated commercial areas: Head toward a 24-hour medical store, metro station entrance, ATM booth with a security guard, or grocery store.
  3. Call the National Emergency Line 112: Inform the operator of your current landmark: “I am being actively followed by a person near [Landmark/Metro Station]. Please dispatch a patrol car.”
  4. Alert people around you loudly: If the person approaches, shout firmly in the local language or Hindi: “Peeche hatiye, aap mera peecha kyu kar rahe hain?” (Back up, why are you following me?) Harassers rely on quiet compliance; public attention often causes them to flee.
How do I set up the Emergency SOS panic feature on my smartphone in India? +

Under the Government of India's Department of Telecommunications mandate, all mobile phones sold in India feature a dedicated hardware panic button that triggers ERSS 112:

For Android Phones (Samsung, Xiaomi, Vivo, Realme, OnePlus, Pixel):

  • Open Settings → Safety & Emergency (or Emergency SOS).
  • Enable Emergency SOS. Pressing the Power button 3 or 5 times rapidly will automatically dial 112 and send SMS alerts with live GPS tracking to your registered emergency contacts.
  • Install the official 112 India App from Google Play Store for the silent "SHOUT" panic trigger.

For Apple iPhones (iOS):

  • Go to Settings → Emergency SOS → Turn on “Call with 5 Button Presses”.
  • In India, Emergency SOS is mapped directly to 112.
  • Go to the Health app → Medical ID → Add your family members as emergency contacts. They will receive automated location updates when SOS is activated.
What is a "Zero FIR", and can an Indian police station refuse to register my complaint? +

No police station can legally refuse to register an FIR for a cognizable crime.

Under Indian criminal law (Supreme Court ruling and Section 154 CrPC / BNSS), a Zero FIR can be registered at any police station across the country, regardless of where the crime took place or territorial jurisdiction.

  • The police station registers the FIR under number "0" and immediately begins preliminary investigation or medical assistance before transferring the case to the appropriate police station.
  • Refusal by a police officer to register an FIR regarding crimes against women is a punishable offence under Section 166A of the Indian Penal Code (IPC), carrying up to 2 years imprisonment.
  • If any station refuses, you can call 112 or file an online complaint directly with the State Police Commissioner or the National Commission for Women (NCW).
What should I do if someone blackmails me with private or morphed photos online? +

Cyber blackmail and non-consensual image sharing are serious criminal offences under the Information Technology Act (Sections 66E, 67, 67A) and the IPC:

  1. Do NOT pay money or delete chats: Paying extortionists never stops blackmail; it invites bigger demands. Keep all message threads as evidence.
  2. Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline: Dial 1930 immediately. Specially trained officers can freeze fraudulent transaction accounts and log complaints.
  3. File on the National Cyber Portal: Visit cybercrime.gov.in and select "Report Crime Against Women & Children". You can report anonymously or file an official FIR.
  4. Block Non-Consensual Image Sharing: Use StopNCII.org to create a cryptographic digital fingerprint (hash) of the images directly on your device. Platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and Threads automatically prevent those images from ever being uploaded.
What is the safest protocol when taking Ola, Uber, or auto-rickshaws at night? +

When travelling late in Indian cities, follow these safety habits:

  • Match Plate & OTP: Always verify the vehicle registration plate number carefully against your app. Never board a vehicle whose number does not match.
  • Never Ask “Are you here for [Your Name]?”: Instead ask: “Kiske liye aaye hain? (Who are you picking up?)” Let the driver confirm your name.
  • Share WhatsApp Live Location: Send your 8-hour live location to family or your partner before the vehicle starts moving.
  • Check Child Locks: Check that the rear door inside handle unlocks and opens properly.
  • For Auto-Rickshaws: Take a photo of the rear registration plate and driver display badge on your phone and send it to your trusted contact.
How does the POSH Act 2013 protect women in workplaces and universities? +

The Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Act 2013 applies to all working women in India across corporate, government, educational, and unorganized sectors:

  • Every organization with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) headed by a senior woman employee and an external NGO member.
  • The definition of sexual harassment covers unwelcome physical contact, demands for sexual favors, sexually colored remarks, showing pornography, and unwelcome verbal or non-verbal conduct.
  • Complaints can be filed within 3 months of the incident. Inquiries must be completed within 90 days.
  • The law strictly penalizes retaliation, intimidation, or negative appraisal against women who file complaints.
Are Indian women helplines like 1091 and 181 free and confidential? +

Yes. Both 1091 and 181 are 100% toll-free and confidential.

They can be called from any mobile network (Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL) or landline across India, even if you have zero balance or no outgoing call package.

  • 1091 (Women in Distress): Operated by state police for immediate emergency response, PCR dispatch, and protection against stalking or physical violence.
  • 181 (Women Helpline): Operated by the Ministry of Women and Child Development for domestic violence, family disputes, counseling, and direct admission to Sakhi One Stop Centres.
Is pepper spray legal for women's self-defense in India? +

Yes, carrying and using pepper spray for genuine self-defense is completely legal in India.

Under Sections 96 to 106 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC) (Right of Private Defence), every citizen has the legal right to defend their body against unlawful harm or sexual assault.

  • Pepper spray is legally classified as a non-lethal self-defense device and does not require an arms license.
  • Keep it in an easily accessible pocket or purse pouch rather than buried at the bottom of your bag.
  • Be aware that pepper spray can be temporarily surrendered or restricted at certain high-security metro checkpoints or airports (carry in checked luggage, not cabin baggage).
